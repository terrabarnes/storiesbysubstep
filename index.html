What these are

Every word is decodable.

A child learning to read through a structured programme can only use the patterns she has been taught. Most books ignore that. These do not.

Every word in these readers is one a reader can sound out with the patterns taught up to her stage, or a high-frequency word introduced by then. Not most words. Every word. Each book is checked against its ceiling, word by word, before it is printed.

That is the ordinary promise of a decodable reader. The unusual part is that these are real stories, with a plot and people in them, and a reason to turn the page.

The five sets follow the substeps of Wilson Reading System® Book 3. The correspondence chart below shows exactly which set lines up with which substep.

Why they sound the way they do

The limits do the writing.

At this stage a reader has not met silent e, or vowel teams, or r-controlled vowels. So a great many ordinary words are simply unavailable: feel, good, see, look, again, hard, sorry, afraid.

Nobody in these books can be nervous, then. Nobody can be afraid, or sorry. What they can be is specific:

Her neck gets hot. Her hands get damp.

ROBIN AND THE RABBIT · SET 1

The constraint turned out to be the best editor on the project. A character who cannot say sorry has to do something instead. A book that cannot use the word lie calls it a fib, which is what a child calls it anyway.

Who is in them

The cast.

Robin
Cannot spell. Hands her test in fast, with the back on top, so nobody can see the blanks. Is the best in the world at getting a rabbit to sit still.
Calvin
Ten out of ten on every test. Gets off the bus at six, unlocks a flat with nobody in it, and does his maths at the desk on his own.
Chomp
A rabbit. Kicks, thumps, and will not sit in a lap for just anyone.
Nick
Picks on Robin on the bus. Cannot do the maths, and does not want anyone to know it.
Gran
Calvin’s grandmother. Owns the lamp, and has firm views about being told the truth.
Sam, Rex and Max
A boy, a dog who will not sit, and a twin brother. Their books are short on purpose, for a reader with not much stamina yet.
