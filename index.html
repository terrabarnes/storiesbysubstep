How they are made

Carefully Controlled. Carefully Written.

Each Stories by Substep book corresponds to a specific Wilson Reading System® instructional substep.

Words are checked against the phonics patterns and permitted high-frequency words at that level. New decoding skills are introduced in small, deliberate increments, while previously learned patterns are reinforced.

This precision gives children the opportunity to apply what they’ve learned without encountering decoding patterns they haven’t yet been taught.

And because reading is about more than decoding, every book is also written to tell a real story — with interesting characters, meaningful problems, and satisfying endings.

The decoding is controlled. The storytelling isn’t an afterthought.

This is the opening of Set 1 — the very first page a child reads. Every word here uses two closed syllables or a sight word taught by that point. Nothing else.

Robin has a rabbit. He is Chomp.

Chomp kicks. He thumps. He will not sit in a lap for just anyone.

But Robin lifts him up, and Chomp sits still in her lap. She rubs the soft spot on his chin. His back legs let go.

Robin can get a rabbit to sit still. Robin is the best at this.

In class, Robin is not the best at anything.

ROBIN AND THE RABBIT · SET 1 · SUBSTEP 3.1

At this level a reader hasn’t met silent e, vowel teams, or r-controlled vowels — so feel, good, afraid and sorry are all unavailable. Nobody in these books can say they feel nervous. What they can be is specific:

Her neck gets hot. Her hands get damp.
